# Notifications, privacy and control

## Choosing what you hear about

Settings lists what OneCare can tell you about, by **thing** rather than by
channel:

- **Medicine reminders** on this device.
- **Missed dose alerts** to the people in your care circle, by email.
- **Your adherence report**, in the app.
- **Account and security** email — this one cannot be switched off.

Two things worth knowing. A setting only appears if something actually sends it,
so there are no switches for mail that does not exist. And the one that cannot
be turned off says so on the row, with the reason: it is how you keep control of
your account, and there is no version of it you would want silenced.

Medicine reminders need two things: the setting here, and your browser or phone
allowing notifications. Both have to be on.

## Who has looked at your record

Your access history shows who opened your record, when, and which parts. It
records reading as well as changing.

This is a report the app makes, not something the database can prove on its own
— nothing in a database knows a page was rendered. What OneCare guarantees is
that a report cannot be forged or attributed to the wrong person; it cannot
guarantee that every read was reported.

## Your data, elsewhere

- **Export everything** from Settings, in a portable format.
- **Send a report** to a clinician as a PDF or CSV without giving them access to
  your account.
- **Delete your account.** Records a clinician created and is legally required
  to keep stay with them — that is the law rather than our choice — but your
  OneCare account and the data you put in it go.

## When something is removed from your record

Your record is yours. OneCare does not delete things from it, and neither can
your clinician — they can withdraw a document they sent you, and that leaves a
note in its place saying what was withdrawn and why.

**There is one exception, and this is the whole of it.**

If a clinician sends you information that belongs to another patient — the
commonest cause is two people with the same name — that information is removed
from your record, along with anything added to your record from it. You are
told when it happens, and it stays in your account's activity log in Settings.

It works that way for two reasons, and only one of them is the law.

The other person's most private information ended up in front of a stranger.
Leaving it there because taking it back is awkward would be choosing our
convenience over their dignity. And you did not ask for somebody else's
diagnosis and should not have to carry it — receiving information about a
stranger's illness is not a neutral thing to happen to you.

Data protection law says the same, in more words: under the UK and EU GDPR an
accidental disclosure is a personal data breach, and the healthcare provider
who made it is required to contain it.

**Nothing else is ever removed this way.** Not a note you disagree with, not a
result you would rather not have, not a record a clinician later regrets. Those
can be corrected, disputed or added to — never quietly taken away.

**If you think something was withdrawn wrongly**, or you want to know more about
why, speak to the healthcare provider who sent it. They hold the record and
they made the decision; we hold the evidence of what happened and can show it to
them, but we are not able to overturn their clinical or professional judgement.
