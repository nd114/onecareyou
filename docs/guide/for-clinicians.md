# For clinicians

## Getting access to a patient

Three ways, and they differ in who decides:

1. **The patient invites you.** They choose the categories; you see those.
2. **The patient shares with your hospital**, and your hospital assigns you.
3. **You create a record** for someone not yet on OneCare — from a visit, or a
   bulk import. When they sign up with the same confirmed email address, they
   are offered the record and can accept or decline it.

Once a patient claims a record it is theirs. You keep your copy; corrections to
their record go through them.

## The chart

Fifteen tabs, and a rail that follows you down all of them carrying Send
guidance, Set alert, and a menu with Start encounter, Add task, Refer, Message
and My notes.

Once the header scrolls away the rail shows the patient's name and risk chip.
That is there so you always know whose chart you are writing in.

## The risk badge

Open it and it tells you the rule that produced the level against this patient's
own counts — not just the findings but why they add up to that level. It also
names what it did **not** weigh: a measurement with no reference range
contributes nothing, and the badge says so rather than letting silence read as
an all-clear.

## Dictation

Record up to ten minutes. Transcription and summary are approved separately, and
nothing reaches a record until you file it.

You can rename a dictation afterwards, and archive one. You cannot delete a
dictation that has been filed — it is part of a chart by then.

## Alerts

Thresholds you set on a patient's readings email you when they are breached.
**These cannot be switched off in notification settings.** You set them because
the reading matters, and a rule that can be muted somewhere else is a rule you
cannot rely on.

## Taking back a document sent in error

If you file a document to the wrong patient — the same name, the wrong Jane
Evans — you can withdraw it. It disappears from their vault and from anyone they
had shared with, immediately.

What it does not do is undo what was already read. The withdrawal records how
long the document was visible and whether it was opened, because that is what
you will need to know when you decide whether it has to be reported.

## The assistant

It proposes; you approve. Nothing is sent or written until you do, every write
runs under your own session so the same access rules apply, and the approval is
logged against your account.

It is decision support. Clinical decisions, and everything written to a record,
remain yours.
